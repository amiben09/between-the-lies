# Production mechanics: what we learned from the reference shorts (general rules only)

Studied on 2026-11-17: 3 Ricks Clips shorts (59.6 s, 65.3 s, 65.0 s; 720x1280 delivered, 30 fps), plus the
engine repo's own 20-short study (`../../references/STYLE_BIBLE.md`, `../../references/reports/`).
Private study only. We copy NO script, character, shot or voice. Only the mechanics below.

## Measured
| Mechanic | Measured | Our rule |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 59.6-65.3 s | 60-70 s |
| Spoken words | ~190 words in 59.6 s (~3.2-3.7 words/s) | 175-195 words to start, 2.8-3.3 words/s (drama breathes more) |
| Hard cuts (ffmpeg scene > 0.3) | 0-1 per video | Max 3 per episode. Use continuous travel instead. |
| Voice start | Frame 1-4, no intro or logo | Same: voice starts within 0.15 s |
| Captions | 1-2 words at a time, white bold sans, black outline, centred at ~70 % height, hard swap on each word | Same mechanics, with our own font and colour (see engine_changes.md) |
| Hook | Sentence 1 states the shocking act. Stakes come before context. | Sentence 1 = the shocking act or the contradiction, about relationships |
| Ending | The video ends on the last word. Twist in the last 1-2 sentences. No moral. | Same |
| Dialogue | Short in-character lines, 2-4 per video | Max 4 |
| Recurring object | One prop carries the story (bell + rope + ring pattern; rail gap; jar balls) | 1 object, at least 3 appearances |
| Light = story time | Night blue for the mystery, pink sunrise for the reveal | A light state per act (see camera_grammar.md) |

## Structural pattern seen in the strongest relationship-style example
1. **Hook:** a disturbing act stated flatly (0-3 s).
2. **Mechanism:** how the world works, which plants the rule the twist will use (3-10 s).
3. **Crowd reaction:** others doubt or mock it, with 1-2 short dialogue lines (10-27 s).
4. **POV shift:** "But [the person closest to it] isn't…". Who is NOT reacting? (27-32 s)
5. **Planted detail pays off:** the specific signal or object, then "Wait, …?" (32-37 s).
6. **Reveal of the hidden motive:** a reframe (37-46 s).
7. **The choice:** the character decides, and we watch them NOT act (46-54 s).
8. **Final line:** a cold, short consequence. The picture's light changes (54-60 s).

The twist works because it was planted at step 2 (the rule) and step 5 (the signal). This is the
"earned twist" standard used in `story_formula.md`.

## Picture mechanics
- Every sentence is illustrated literally. The image changes on the key noun of each clause.
- The camera travels between subjects (follow a rope, a gaze, a light beam) instead of cutting.
- Holds always creep (a push-in). Nothing is static for 4 frames or more.
- Foreground framing (a window frame, a doorway, a prop near the lens) in about 1/3 of shots.
- Close-ups for reactions. Insert shots for the object (the bell, the note on the wall).
- Lighting tints carry mood: candle-warm interiors against moon-blue exteriors.

## What we deliberately do differently
- Drama pacing: slower, with 1-2 deliberate silences of 0.4-0.8 s (the reference has almost none).
- Captions highlight 1 key emotional word per sentence in our accent colour.
- No comedy exaggeration beats. Tension comes from the gap between what we see and what we know.
- Our own original characters, palette, music and narrator.
