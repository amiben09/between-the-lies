## 1. The Monster She Made Out of Jealousy — Scylla (Circe)
- Core: Circe ↔ Scylla, the nymph who took the man Circe loved. Wound: Circe, jealous, poisons Scylla's bathing pool with her herbs — and creates the six-headed monster that will eat sailors for centuries.
- Hook: "Every sailor who died in Scylla's jaws was killed by one jealous girl."
- Twist: the narrator is the monster's maker; at the end Circe faces Scylla and turns her to stone — an act of guilt and mercy.
- Visuals: herbs dropped into a glowing pool · six heads rising from the water · stone settling over the creature.
- World: "Greek gods and nymphs live beside mortals; a few witches can change any body with herbs." Theme fit: a monster literally MADE.
## 2. His Father's Curse Came True — Maeglin
- Core: Maeglin ↔ Idril (his cousin, a love forbidden among elves) and Eöl, his possessive father. Wound: Eöl kills Maeglin's mother with a javelin meant for him; condemned, Eöl curses his son to die the same death — thrown from the walls.
- Hook: "His father cursed him to die by falling. He spent his life climbing toward the one woman he couldn't have."
- Twist: captured by the Dark Lord, he betrays the hidden city for the promise of Idril — and dies thrown from its walls, exactly as cursed.
- Visuals: a dark forest of no sunlight · the white hidden city in a ring of mountains · a fall from the walls into fire.
## 3. The Forest Was Only a Grieving Queen — the Wood Queen (Uprooted)
- Core: a village girl ↔ the corrupting Wood. Twist: the Wood's heart is a queen whose people were betrayed and who was buried alive in a tree by a human king; her grief became the forest that eats villages.
- Visuals: a heart-tree with a face inside the bark · white-eyed villagers · the girl laying her hand on the bark.
## 4. She Fed the Monster Who Was Her Brother — Asterion (Ariadne)
- Core: Ariadne ↔ her half-brother, the Minotaur, locked in the labyrinth by their father. Wound: born from a god's punishment of their mother; caged and fed prisoners.
- Twist: the princess who gives Theseus the thread is helping kill the only family member who never lied to her — then Theseus abandons her.
- Visuals: a red thread unspooling in the dark · a horned child's silhouette · an empty beach on Naxos. (Novel details to verify.)
## 5. The Friend Who Pulled the Trigger — Letty Price (Babel)
- Core: Letty ↔ Robin and Ramy, the friends of colour she studied magic-translation with at Oxford. Wound: grief for her brother, the need to be the "good" daughter of the Empire.
- Twist: the friend who said she was on their side shoots Ramy. Explain without excusing. World: "In 1830s Oxford, translators craft silver bars whose magic powers the British Empire."
## 6. He Married the Girl Who Forgot Her Name — Túrin (The Children of Húrin)
- Core: Túrin ↔ his sister Nienor, who never met him. A dragon's spell erases her memory; they fall in love and marry. Twist: the dragon reveals the truth as it dies. ⚠ A: battles must stay off-screen.
## 7. The Brother He Killed Was My Brother — Cassius (Red Rising)
- Core: Cassius ↔ Darrow, his best friend at the academy, who secretly killed Cassius's brother Julian in the entrance trial. ⚠ The Paramount film will soon create a competing look.
## 8. The Grey King Only Wanted His Family Back — Luciano Anatolius (Lies of Locke Lamora)
- Core: a man whose family was murdered by the city's crime boss returns as a masked killer. ⚠ Story revolves around a heist plot — harder to compress.
## 9. The Servant Who Became God — Rashek (Mistborn)
- Core: a servant who kills the prophesied hero and takes his power, becoming the immortal tyrant for 1,000 years. ⚠ W: metal-magic world is heavy.
## 10. The Boy Who Planned the Perfect Murder — Henry Winter (The Secret History)
- Core: a brilliant student who leads his friends into killing one of their own. ⚠ Depth 3: the book gives little "made" backstory.

## Recommendation
**#1 Scylla (Circe)** — the channel theme made literal (a monster created by a person), 2 characters + 1 object (the herbs),
a twist that turns the narrator into the culprit, and pure "imagination" visuals: glowing herbs, a sea-cave, six heads.
**#2 Maeglin** — the strongest curse-payoff structure; zero screen adaptations at all.
**#3 Wood Queen** — a beautiful visual world (a forest with a face); the twist re-frames the villain completely.
